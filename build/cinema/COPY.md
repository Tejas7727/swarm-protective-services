# Copy — the home film

Written with `brand-copy`: mine the customer's words, write the spine, ten candidates
per headline, score them, cut 30%, read aloud, count.

**Budgets.** Hero headline 2–6 words · line ≤ 14 words · everything on one stop ≤ 25
words · whole film ≤ 180 words. This film: **123 words** above the answers section.

## What customers actually say

Verbatim, from reviews and forums for security companies and venues:

- "flawless security and were extremely professional"
- "kept clients informed of incidents and reacted swiftly"
- "a written security plan and a named operations contact"
- "guests don't even realise security is present"
- and the fear, said plainly: "the bouncers were attacking people and not focused on
  safeguarding" — buyers are as afraid of the guards as of the crowd

So the two jobs of this copy: **size deters**, and **training means nobody ends up on
camera**. Every headline serves one of them.

## The spine (read it alone — it should already make someone call)

1. One call puts a trained team on your night.
2. Trouble is turned away at the door, politely.
3. When it starts, it ends before anyone films it.
4. Door to car, the route was walked first.
5. By morning: a written report, nothing to report.
6. Tell us the night. We staff it.

## STOP 0 — `call` — big people, calm training, one call away

```
Headline: Big on purpose. Calm by training.            score 11/12
Line:     Licensed door staff, event security and close protection across the GTA.  (11 words)
Ask:      What needs covering? · A venue · An event · A person
CTA:      Get a quote · Call dispatch
Runner-up: Trouble takes one look.                     score 10/12
```
**Why it works:** it answers both fears in six words — the size is deliberate, the
behaviour is trained — and it is the only line on the page a competitor could not
paste their name onto.
Rejected: "Hire us. Forget about it." (10 — reads mob, not professional) ·
"One call. Then nothing happens." (10) · "The door is ours. The night is yours." (10) ·
"Your night, handled." (6, generic) · "Nobody argues with the door." (9, overclaims).

## STOP 1 — `venues` — the night is decided at the door

```
Headline: Most trouble never gets in.                  score 10/12
Line:     IDs checked by hand. Headcount kept all night.   (8 words)
  venue   IDs checked by hand. Headcount kept all night.
  event   Every name against your list. Gatecrashers turned around.
  person  Your way in is walked before you arrive.
Runner-up: Stopped at the door, not the dance floor.    score 10/12
```
**Why it works:** "most" is honest, and the line is two verifiable door tasks instead
of an adjective. AGCO capacity and ID checks are the two things that actually cost a
venue its licence.

## STOP 2 — `events` — it ends before anyone films it

```
Headline: Nothing worth filming.                       score 11/12
Line:     De-escalation first, refreshed twice a year, not once at hire.  (10 words)
  venue   Two officers, two angles, and a quiet word first.
  event   Guests never meet the problem that was already handled.
  person  Someone watches the room so you do not have to.
Runner-up: Over before the phones come out.            score 10/12
```
**Why it works:** every venue owner in 2026 is afraid of one clip going up. The line
is the proof: refresher training twice a year is a fact competitors do not publish.

## STOP 3 — `protection` — the route was walked before you arrived

```
Headline: The exit is planned before the entrance.     score 11/12
Line:     Close protection from the first door to your own.   (9 words)
  venue   Your staff walked to their cars at close.
  event   The people the night is about leave quietly.
  person  Close protection from the first door to your own.
Runner-up: Every exit, already walked.                 score 11/12
```
**Why it works:** it describes advance work — the part of close protection that
separates a professional detail from a large friend — without one adjective.

## STOP 4 — `report` — the night ends in writing

```
Headline: You slept. We wrote it down.                 score 11/12
Line:     Written before the crew leaves your building.      (7 words)
Card:     Shift report — sample · Refused at the door 4 · Settled without contact 1 ·
          Injuries 0 · Police called 0 · Filed 06:04, signed by the lead
Runner-up: Nothing to report. In writing.              score 11/12
```
**Why it works:** the reader is the hero and asleep; the service is the paperwork that
was happening anyway. The card is labelled *sample* because we do not fake records.

## STOP 5 — `book` — tell us the night

```
Headline: Forget about it. We won't.                   score 10/12
Line:     Tell us the date and the place. You get a number the same day.  (13 words)
CTA:      Request a quote · (647) 555-0173 · Instagram · TikTok · email
Runner-up: Call the swarm.                             score 10/12
```
**Why it works:** it is the client's own sentence — "hire us and forget about it" —
turned into a promise with a two-word twist, and it lands on the only word that
matters at the end of a sale.

"in today's world" · "At Swarm, we…" · any three-adjective list · fear as a sales
lever · any implication of violence · any armed-bodyguard claim (unlawful in Ontario).
